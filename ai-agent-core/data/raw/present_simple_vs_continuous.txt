PRESENT SIMPLE VS PRESENT CONTINUOUS

CHAPTER 1: PRESENT SIMPLE TENSE

1.1 Form and Structure

The Present Simple tense is one of the most fundamental tenses in English. It follows a straightforward structure:

Affirmative: Subject + base verb (+ s/es for third person singular)
- I/You/We/They work every day.
- He/She/It works every day.

Negative: Subject + do/does + not + base verb
- I do not (don't) like coffee.
- She does not (doesn't) like coffee.

Question: Do/Does + subject + base verb?
- Do you speak English?
- Does he speak English?

Important spelling rules for third person singular (he/she/it):
- Most verbs: add -s (work → works, play → plays, read → reads)
- Verbs ending in -s, -sh, -ch, -x, -z, -o: add -es (watch → watches, go → goes, wash → washes)
- Verbs ending in consonant + y: change y to i and add -es (study → studies, carry → carries)
- Irregular: have → has

1.2 Usage

The Present Simple is used in the following situations:

a) Habits and routines:
- I wake up at 7 o'clock every morning.
- She drinks coffee before work.
- They go to the gym three times a week.

b) General truths and facts:
- Water boils at 100 degrees Celsius.
- The Earth revolves around the Sun.
- Cats are mammals.

c) Permanent situations:
- He lives in London.
- I work at a bank.
- She speaks three languages.

d) Scheduled events and timetables:
- The train departs at 9:15 AM.
- The movie starts at 8 PM tonight.
- School begins on September 1st.

e) Instructions and directions:
- You turn left at the traffic light, then go straight.
- First, you mix the flour and sugar together.

1.3 Signal Words

Common time expressions used with Present Simple: always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never, every day, every week, every month, every year, on Mondays, twice a week, once a month, in the morning, at night.


CHAPTER 2: PRESENT CONTINUOUS TENSE

2.1 Form and Structure

The Present Continuous (also called Present Progressive) uses the following structure:

Affirmative: Subject + am/is/are + verb-ing
- I am working right now.
- He/She/It is working right now.
- You/We/They are working right now.

Negative: Subject + am/is/are + not + verb-ing
- I am not (I'm not) sleeping.
- He is not (isn't) sleeping.
- They are not (aren't) sleeping.

Question: Am/Is/Are + subject + verb-ing?
- Are you listening to me?
- Is she coming to the party?

Spelling rules for adding -ing:
- Most verbs: add -ing (work → working, play → playing, read → reading)
- Verbs ending in -e: drop -e and add -ing (make → making, write → writing, come → coming)
- Short verbs ending in consonant-vowel-consonant: double the last consonant (run → running, sit → sitting, stop → stopping)
- Verbs ending in -ie: change -ie to -y and add -ing (die → dying, lie → lying, tie → tying)

2.2 Usage

The Present Continuous is used in the following situations:

a) Actions happening right now, at the moment of speaking:
- I am reading a book right now.
- She is talking on the phone at the moment.
- Look! The children are playing in the garden.

b) Temporary situations:
- I am staying with my friend this week.
- He is working at a café until he finds a better job.
- We are living in a small apartment for now.

c) Changing or developing situations:
- The weather is getting colder.
- Technology is changing rapidly.
- My English is improving.

d) Future arrangements (with a definite time or plan):
- I am meeting John tomorrow at 3 PM.
- We are flying to Paris next Monday.
- She is starting a new job next week.

e) Repeated annoying actions (with always):
- He is always leaving his clothes on the floor!
- She is always interrupting me when I speak!
- You are always forgetting your keys!

2.3 Signal Words

Common time expressions used with Present Continuous: now, right now, at the moment, currently, at present, today, this week, this month, these days, tonight, Look!, Listen!


CHAPTER 3: KEY DIFFERENCES

Understanding when to use each tense is crucial for clear communication in English.

3.1 Permanent vs Temporary

Present Simple (permanent): I live in New York. (This is my home.)
Present Continuous (temporary): I am living in New York this summer. (Only for a short time.)

Present Simple (permanent): She works at a hospital. (Her regular job.)
Present Continuous (temporary): She is working at a hospital this month. (Temporary assignment.)

3.2 Habits vs Current Actions

Present Simple (habit): I drink tea every morning. (Regular habit.)
Present Continuous (now): I am drinking tea right now. (At this exact moment.)

Present Simple (routine): He reads before bed. (Every night.)
Present Continuous (now): He is reading a novel. (Currently, in progress.)

3.3 Facts vs Changing Situations

Present Simple (fact): The population of the world is about 8 billion.
Present Continuous (change): The population of the world is increasing.

3.4 Stative Verbs

Some verbs are typically NOT used in the continuous form because they describe states, not actions. These are called stative verbs:

Mental states: know, believe, understand, remember, forget, think (opinion), suppose, realize
Emotions: love, hate, like, dislike, prefer, want, need, wish
Senses: see, hear, smell, taste, feel (when describing a state)
Possession: have (possession), own, belong, possess
Other: be, seem, appear, contain, consist, cost, mean, matter

Incorrect: I am knowing the answer.
Correct: I know the answer.

Incorrect: She is loving chocolate.
Correct: She loves chocolate.

Note: Some stative verbs can be used in continuous form when they describe an action, not a state:
- I think you are right. (opinion — state)
- I am thinking about my vacation. (mental activity — action)
- He has a car. (possession — state)
- He is having lunch. (eating — action)


CHAPTER 4: COMMON MISTAKES

Mistake 1: Using Present Continuous for habits
Incorrect: I am going to school every day.
Correct: I go to school every day.

Mistake 2: Using Present Simple for current actions
Incorrect: Look! She runs in the park.
Correct: Look! She is running in the park.

Mistake 3: Forgetting the -s for third person singular
Incorrect: He work at a bank.
Correct: He works at a bank.

Mistake 4: Forgetting the auxiliary verb in Present Continuous
Incorrect: She working right now.
Correct: She is working right now.

Mistake 5: Using continuous with stative verbs
Incorrect: I am understanding the lesson now.
Correct: I understand the lesson now.

Mistake 6: Confusing permanent and temporary situations
Incorrect: I live in a hotel this week. (sounds permanent)
Correct: I am living in a hotel this week. (temporary)

Mistake 7: Wrong negative form
Incorrect: He doesn't works here.
Correct: He doesn't work here.


CHAPTER 5: PRACTICE SENTENCES

Fill in the blank with the correct form (Present Simple or Present Continuous):

1. She usually _____ (wake up) at 6 AM, but today she _____ (sleep) late.
   Answer: wakes up / is sleeping

2. Water _____ (freeze) at 0 degrees Celsius.
   Answer: freezes

3. Listen! Someone _____ (knock) on the door.
   Answer: is knocking

4. My father _____ (work) in an office. He _____ (not work) on weekends.
   Answer: works / doesn't work

5. Right now, we _____ (study) English grammar.
   Answer: are studying

6. The sun _____ (rise) in the east and _____ (set) in the west.
   Answer: rises / sets

7. Be quiet! The baby _____ (sleep).
   Answer: is sleeping

8. He _____ (always / complain) about the weather!
   Answer: is always complaining

9. I _____ (not understand) this question. Can you help me?
   Answer: don't understand

10. She _____ (fly) to Tokyo next Friday. Her tickets are already booked.
    Answer: is flying
