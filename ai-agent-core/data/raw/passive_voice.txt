PASSIVE VOICE IN ENGLISH

CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION TO PASSIVE VOICE

In English, sentences can be written in two voices: active voice and passive voice.

Active voice: The subject performs the action.
- The cat ate the fish.

Passive voice: The subject receives the action.
- The fish was eaten by the cat.

The passive voice is formed with the verb "to be" + past participle (V3).

General formula:
Active: Subject + verb + object
Passive: Object (becomes new subject) + "to be" + past participle + (by + agent)


CHAPTER 2: PASSIVE VOICE IN DIFFERENT TENSES

2.1 Present Simple Passive

Active: Subject + verb(s/es) + object
Passive: Subject + am/is/are + past participle

Examples:
Active: The company produces cars.
Passive: Cars are produced by the company.

Active: She cleans the office every day.
Passive: The office is cleaned every day.

Active: They speak English in many countries.
Passive: English is spoken in many countries.

Active: The teacher corrects the homework.
Passive: The homework is corrected by the teacher.

2.2 Past Simple Passive

Active: Subject + verb (V2) + object
Passive: Subject + was/were + past participle

Examples:
Active: Shakespeare wrote Romeo and Juliet.
Passive: Romeo and Juliet was written by Shakespeare.

Active: They built the bridge in 1990.
Passive: The bridge was built in 1990.

Active: Someone stole my bicycle yesterday.
Passive: My bicycle was stolen yesterday.

Active: The police arrested the suspect.
Passive: The suspect was arrested by the police.

2.3 Present Perfect Passive

Active: Subject + have/has + past participle + object
Passive: Subject + have/has + been + past participle

Examples:
Active: The government has approved the new law.
Passive: The new law has been approved by the government.

Active: Scientists have discovered a new species.
Passive: A new species has been discovered by scientists.

Active: Someone has broken the window.
Passive: The window has been broken.

Active: They have cancelled the flight.
Passive: The flight has been cancelled.

2.4 Future Simple Passive

Active: Subject + will + verb + object
Passive: Subject + will be + past participle

Examples:
Active: They will announce the results tomorrow.
Passive: The results will be announced tomorrow.

Active: The team will complete the project next month.
Passive: The project will be completed next month.

Active: The company will deliver the package on Monday.
Passive: The package will be delivered on Monday.

2.5 Present Continuous Passive

Active: Subject + am/is/are + verb-ing + object
Passive: Subject + am/is/are + being + past participle

Examples:
Active: They are renovating the building.
Passive: The building is being renovated.

Active: The mechanic is repairing my car.
Passive: My car is being repaired.

2.6 Past Continuous Passive

Active: Subject + was/were + verb-ing + object
Passive: Subject + was/were + being + past participle

Examples:
Active: The workers were painting the walls.
Passive: The walls were being painted.

Active: She was preparing the presentation.
Passive: The presentation was being prepared.

2.7 Modal Verbs in Passive

Active: Subject + modal + verb + object
Passive: Subject + modal + be + past participle

Examples:
Active: You must submit the report by Friday.
Passive: The report must be submitted by Friday.

Active: They can solve this problem easily.
Passive: This problem can be solved easily.

Active: We should inform the customers.
Passive: The customers should be informed.

Active: You may use this room for meetings.
Passive: This room may be used for meetings.


CHAPTER 3: WHEN TO USE PASSIVE VOICE

The passive voice is not just a grammatical structure; it serves specific communication purposes.

3.1 When the Agent is Unknown
- My wallet was stolen. (We don't know who stole it.)
- The window was broken. (We don't know who broke it.)
- A new hospital has been built downtown. (The builders are not important.)

3.2 When the Agent is Obvious
- The suspect was arrested. (Obviously by the police.)
- The patient was examined carefully. (Obviously by the doctor.)
- The mail is delivered every morning. (Obviously by the mail carrier.)

3.3 When the Action is More Important Than the Agent
- The Mona Lisa was painted in the early 16th century.
- Penicillin was discovered in 1928.
- The Olympic Games are held every four years.

3.4 In Formal, Scientific, and Academic Writing
- The experiment was conducted under controlled conditions.
- The data were analyzed using statistical methods.
- It has been proven that exercise reduces stress.
- The results are presented in Table 3.

3.5 In News Reports
- Three people were injured in the accident.
- The new law was passed by parliament yesterday.
- A historic agreement has been reached between the two countries.

3.6 To Be Polite or Tactful
- A mistake was made. (Avoids blaming someone directly.)
- The deadline was missed. (More diplomatic than "You missed the deadline.")
- The vase was broken. (Instead of "You broke the vase.")


CHAPTER 4: "BY" + AGENT

The agent (the person or thing performing the action) is introduced with "by." The agent is included only when it provides important or relevant information.

Include the agent when:
- The agent is important or surprising: The novel was written by a 12-year-old girl.
- The agent provides new information: The award was presented by the president herself.
- The agent needs to be identified: The project was managed by Ms. Chen.

Omit the agent when:
- The agent is unknown: My car was stolen. (By whom? Unknown.)
- The agent is obvious: The suspect was arrested. (By the police — obvious.)
- The agent is unimportant: English is spoken worldwide. (By people — not important.)
- The agent is general: Rice is grown in Asia. (By farmers — general.)


CHAPTER 5: CHANGING ACTIVE TO PASSIVE — STEP BY STEP

Step 1: Identify the subject, verb, and object in the active sentence.
Step 2: Move the object to the subject position.
Step 3: Change the verb to the appropriate passive form (to be + past participle).
Step 4: Move the original subject to the end with "by" (optional).

Example:
Active: The chef (subject) prepared (verb) a delicious meal (object).
Step 1: Subject = The chef, Verb = prepared, Object = a delicious meal
Step 2: A delicious meal...
Step 3: A delicious meal was prepared...
Step 4: A delicious meal was prepared by the chef.

Example with Present Perfect:
Active: Scientists have discovered a new planet.
Passive: A new planet has been discovered by scientists.

Example with Future:
Active: The company will launch the product next month.
Passive: The product will be launched next month.


CHAPTER 6: COMMON MISTAKES

Mistake 1: Forgetting the past participle
Incorrect: The letter was write yesterday.
Correct: The letter was written yesterday.

Mistake 2: Using the wrong form of "to be"
Incorrect: The cars is manufactured in Germany.
Correct: The cars are manufactured in Germany.

Mistake 3: Using passive with intransitive verbs
Intransitive verbs (verbs without an object) cannot be made passive.
Incorrect: The accident was happened yesterday.
Correct: The accident happened yesterday.
Other intransitive verbs: arrive, go, come, die, sleep, exist, appear, fall

Mistake 4: Double passive
Incorrect: The project was been completed.
Correct: The project has been completed. (Present Perfect Passive)
Correct: The project was completed. (Past Simple Passive)

Mistake 5: Confusing active and passive meaning
Active: The teacher surprised the students. (Teacher = doer)
Passive: The students were surprised by the teacher. (Students = receiver)
Wrong interpretation: "The students were surprised" does NOT mean the students did the surprising.

Mistake 6: Forgetting "been" in perfect passive
Incorrect: The work has finished.
Correct: The work has been finished.
(Note: "The work has finished" is grammatically possible if "finish" is intransitive, but the meaning changes.)

Mistake 7: Using "by" unnecessarily
Weak: The email was sent by someone.
Better: The email was sent.

Mistake 8: Overusing passive voice
While passive voice is useful, overusing it makes writing unclear and wordy. Use active voice when the doer of the action is important and known.


CHAPTER 7: PASSIVE VOICE SUMMARY TABLE

Tense                  | Active                    | Passive
Present Simple         | She writes emails.        | Emails are written.
Past Simple            | She wrote emails.         | Emails were written.
Present Perfect        | She has written emails.    | Emails have been written.
Future Simple          | She will write emails.     | Emails will be written.
Present Continuous     | She is writing emails.     | Emails are being written.
Past Continuous        | She was writing emails.    | Emails were being written.
Modal (can)            | She can write emails.      | Emails can be written.
Modal (must)           | She must write emails.     | Emails must be written.
Modal (should)         | She should write emails.   | Emails should be written.


CHAPTER 8: PRACTICE SENTENCES

Change the following sentences from active to passive:

1. Active: The students completed the assignment.
   Passive: The assignment was completed by the students.

2. Active: Someone has stolen my phone.
   Passive: My phone has been stolen.

3. Active: They will publish the results next week.
   Passive: The results will be published next week.

4. Active: The government is building a new highway.
   Passive: A new highway is being built by the government.

5. Active: People speak Portuguese in Brazil.
   Passive: Portuguese is spoken in Brazil.

6. Active: They must finish the report by Friday.
   Passive: The report must be finished by Friday.

7. Active: Alexander Fleming discovered penicillin in 1928.
   Passive: Penicillin was discovered by Alexander Fleming in 1928.

8. Active: The company has offered her a job.
   Passive: She has been offered a job by the company.

9. Active: Nobody can solve this problem.
   Passive: This problem cannot be solved.

10. Active: The waiter was serving dinner when the fire alarm rang.
    Passive: Dinner was being served when the fire alarm rang.
