PAST SIMPLE VS PRESENT PERFECT

CHAPTER 1: PAST SIMPLE TENSE

1.1 Form and Structure

The Past Simple tense describes completed actions in the past.

Affirmative: Subject + verb in past form (V2)
- Regular verbs: add -ed (worked, played, studied)
- Irregular verbs: use the second form (went, saw, bought, wrote)

Negative: Subject + did not (didn't) + base verb
- I didn't go to the party last night.
- She didn't finish her homework yesterday.

Question: Did + subject + base verb?
- Did you see the movie last weekend?
- Did he call you yesterday?

Spelling rules for regular verbs:
- Most verbs: add -ed (work → worked, clean → cleaned, rain → rained)
- Verbs ending in -e: add -d (live → lived, decide → decided, hope → hoped)
- Verbs ending in consonant + y: change y to i and add -ed (study → studied, carry → carried, try → tried)
- Short verbs ending in consonant-vowel-consonant: double the last consonant and add -ed (stop → stopped, plan → planned, drop → dropped)

Common irregular verbs (must be memorized):
- go → went, come → came, see → saw, do → did, have → had
- make → made, take → took, give → gave, get → got, know → knew
- think → thought, buy → bought, bring → brought, teach → taught
- write → wrote, read → read, speak → spoke, eat → ate, drink → drank
- begin → began, break → broke, choose → chose, drive → drove, fall → fell
- find → found, fly → flew, forget → forgot, grow → grew, hide → hid
- leave → left, lose → lost, meet → met, pay → paid, run → ran
- say → said, sell → sold, send → sent, sit → sat, sleep → slept
- spend → spent, stand → stood, swim → swam, tell → told, win → won

1.2 Usage

The Past Simple is used for:

a) Completed actions at a specific time in the past:
- I visited my grandmother last Sunday.
- They moved to Canada in 2019.
- She graduated from university three years ago.

b) A series of completed actions in the past:
- I woke up, took a shower, and had breakfast.
- He arrived at the office, turned on his computer, and started working.

c) Past habits (often with "used to"):
- When I was young, I played outside every day.
- She lived in Paris for five years.

d) Past states:
- The weather was beautiful yesterday.
- I knew the answer but I didn't say anything.

1.3 Signal Words

Common time expressions: yesterday, last night, last week, last month, last year, ago (two days ago, a week ago), in 2020, in the 1990s, when I was a child, on Monday, the day before yesterday, this morning (if morning is over).


CHAPTER 2: PRESENT PERFECT TENSE

2.1 Form and Structure

The Present Perfect connects the past to the present.

Affirmative: Subject + have/has + past participle (V3)
- I/You/We/They have finished the project.
- He/She/It has finished the project.

Negative: Subject + have/has + not + past participle
- I haven't seen that movie.
- She hasn't arrived yet.

Question: Have/Has + subject + past participle?
- Have you ever been to Japan?
- Has she called you today?

Note: For regular verbs, V2 and V3 are the same (worked, played, studied). For irregular verbs, V3 may be different from V2 (go → went → gone, see → saw → seen, write → wrote → written).

Common irregular past participles (V3):
- be → been, begin → begun, break → broken, choose → chosen, do → done
- drink → drunk, drive → driven, eat → eaten, fall → fallen, fly → flown
- forget → forgotten, give → given, go → gone, grow → grown, hide → hidden
- know → known, ride → ridden, run → run, see → seen, speak → spoken
- swim → swum, take → taken, wear → worn, write → written

2.2 Usage

The Present Perfect is used for:

a) Life experiences (without specifying when):
- I have visited Paris three times.
- She has never eaten sushi.
- Have you ever seen a shooting star?

b) Recent actions with present results:
- I have lost my keys. (I can't find them now.)
- He has broken his leg. (His leg is broken now.)
- She has just finished her exam. (The exam is freshly completed.)

c) Unfinished time periods (today, this week, this year):
- I have drunk three cups of coffee today.
- We have had two meetings this week.
- She has read ten books this year.

d) Actions that started in the past and continue to the present (with for/since):
- I have lived here for ten years. (I still live here.)
- She has worked at this company since 2018. (She still works there.)
- We have known each other since childhood. (We still know each other.)

e) Changes over time:
- My English has improved a lot recently.
- The city has changed dramatically over the past decade.

2.3 Signal Words

Common time expressions: ever, never, just, already, yet, recently, lately, so far, up to now, until now, since (since Monday, since 2020, since I was a child), for (for two years, for a long time, for ages), today, this week, this month, this year, how long, still.

Important distinctions:
- "since" marks a starting point in time (since 2010, since Monday)
- "for" indicates a duration of time (for three hours, for two weeks)
- "already" is used in affirmative sentences (I have already eaten.)
- "yet" is used in negatives and questions (Have you finished yet? I haven't finished yet.)
- "just" means a very short time ago (She has just arrived.)


CHAPTER 3: KEY DIFFERENCES

The critical difference between Past Simple and Present Perfect is the connection to the present.

3.1 Specific Time vs No Specific Time

Past Simple (specific time): I saw that movie last Friday.
Present Perfect (no specific time): I have seen that movie. (When is not important.)

Past Simple: She visited London in 2022.
Present Perfect: She has visited London. (At some point in her life.)

3.2 Finished vs Unfinished Time

Past Simple (finished time period): I ate two apples yesterday. (Yesterday is finished.)
Present Perfect (unfinished time period): I have eaten two apples today. (Today is not finished.)

Past Simple: She wrote three emails this morning. (It is now afternoon.)
Present Perfect: She has written three emails this morning. (It is still morning.)

3.3 Past Situation vs Present Result

Past Simple (focus on past): I lost my wallet. (Telling a story about the past.)
Present Perfect (focus on present result): I have lost my wallet. (I need help finding it now.)

Past Simple: She broke her arm. (Describing what happened.)
Present Perfect: She has broken her arm. (Her arm is currently broken.)

3.4 Completed vs Ongoing

Past Simple (action is finished): I lived in Tokyo for three years. (I don't live there anymore.)
Present Perfect (action continues): I have lived in Tokyo for three years. (I still live there now.)


CHAPTER 4: COMMON MISTAKES

Mistake 1: Using Present Perfect with a specific past time
Incorrect: I have seen that movie yesterday.
Correct: I saw that movie yesterday.

Mistake 2: Using Past Simple for experiences without time
Incorrect: Did you ever visit Paris?
Correct: Have you ever visited Paris?

Mistake 3: Confusing "for" and "since"
Incorrect: I have lived here since five years.
Correct: I have lived here for five years.
Correct: I have lived here since 2020.

Mistake 4: Using Past Simple for unfinished time periods
Incorrect: I ate three meals so far today.
Correct: I have eaten three meals so far today.

Mistake 5: Using wrong past participle form
Incorrect: I have went to the store.
Correct: I have gone to the store.

Mistake 6: Forgetting have/has
Incorrect: She already finished her work.
Correct: She has already finished her work.

Mistake 7: Using Present Perfect when the time period is clearly over
Incorrect: I have seen a great movie last night.
Correct: I saw a great movie last night.


CHAPTER 5: PRACTICE SENTENCES

Choose the correct tense (Past Simple or Present Perfect):

1. I _____ (visit) my grandparents last weekend.
   Answer: visited (specific past time)

2. She _____ (never / try) Indian food before.
   Answer: has never tried (life experience)

3. We _____ (live) in this house since 2015.
   Answer: have lived (started in past, continues now)

4. He _____ (break) his phone yesterday.
   Answer: broke (specific past time)

5. _____ you _____ (finish) your homework yet?
   Answer: Have you finished (yet = present perfect)

6. They _____ (get) married in 2018.
   Answer: got (specific past year)

7. I _____ (just / receive) an email from my boss.
   Answer: have just received (recent action)

8. When _____ you _____ (start) learning English?
   Answer: did you start (asking about specific time)

9. She _____ (work) at this company for ten years.
   Answer: has worked (still working there)

10. I _____ (read) three books this month.
    Answer: have read (this month is not finished)
